# The Trader and the Account Manager shake hands on every trade (design choices entry 81)

Decided by Jacob on 2026-11-14, in his words:
- *"The trader agent will get the list of stocks to trade as part of the work order. It will place one trade at a time, then update the accountmanager the status when the order goes thru. So the accountmanager can update the avail funds (subtract the spent amount) and update the portfolio. Then, pass the trader the approval to make the next trade. This handshaking is between each trade so the Trader can not overdrawn the available funds."*
- *"The trader does not have its own cash copy. It uses the Postgres database with the AccountManager for ACID properties and data integrity."*
- *"Do not reserve any cash. Any available funds in the portfolio can be used."*

## The principle

The Account Manager and its Postgres book are the ONLY owner of the funds and the positions. The Trader holds no cash and no portfolio: not a copy, not a running total, not a snapshot it updates. What the Trader may spend is whatever the book says is available at the moment of the trade. Nothing is reserved for a later purchase: the first trades use the funds, and a later trade gets what is left.

## Today (what is wrong with it, and where it lives)

`prometheus/agents/trader/`:
- `broker.py` `PaperBroker.portfolio` is a COPY of the account, kept up to date with `apply_fill` as the broker fills trades; `affordable_shares(self.portfolio.cash_avail, ...)` decides how many shares the cash pays for; the shares a sale may sell come from the copy too. That is the Trader's own cash.
- `execute.py` takes the portfolio once (`account.trading_portfolio`, step 2), applies "the unbooked fills of an earlier run" to it (`_with_earlier_fills`), runs every trade against the copy (steps 6 and 7), and only at the END tells the Account Manager, once, in one call (`Notifier`, step 8: `verify_trades`, then `account.notify_trades(ctx.start, all the fills, ...)`; with a finding of the guard NOTHING is booked). Between the first fill and that call the book does not know about any of the trades.
- `Run.portfolio_before` / `portfolio_after` and the `cash_before` / `cash_after` of the notifier's answer are worked out from those copies.

`prometheus/agents/account_manager/account_manager.py`: `trading_portfolio` (reads the book when connected, else the portfolio given), `perform_trades` (hands the order to the Trader), `notify_trades` (applies and books a LIST of fills with `apply_fill` and `record_trade`). The book's `record_trade` (`persistence/accounts.py`, `sql/prometheus_accounts.sql`) is already ONE transaction: it locks the account row (`FOR UPDATE`), checks that the account is active, that the cash covers a purchase and that the shares are held, and moves the cash and the position together. That transaction is the guard that cannot be raced: the handshake below puts every trade through it, one at a time.

## The protocol (one trade at a time)

The work order carries the decided trades (`order.trade_decisions` that say `yes_auto` or `yes_user`; the Trader makes its tickets from them, as it does today). Then, for each trade, in the order the broker places them (every sale before any purchase, funding sales before their purchase):

1. **Approval.** The Trader asks the Account Manager for the approval to make THIS trade: `account.approve_trade(trade, price)`. The Account Manager reads the CURRENT available funds and holdings from the book (a database read: `list_portfolio`; with no saved account, from its own working portfolio, see below), and answers an `Approval` (a small pydantic record, `extra="forbid"`): `approved` (bool), `shares` (the shares it approves), `cash_avail` (what the book says is available now), `reason`. For a purchase: the shares are the decision's shares cut to the whole shares the available cash pays for at `price` (`trade_amount`, the account book's rounding, commission as the Trader's config says); none affordable means not approved (`insufficient_cash`). For a sale: the shares held and sellable today (T+1) up to the decision's. It also says no when the account is not active or the decision no longer permits the trade (`work_order.verify_trade`). The Account Manager decides; the Trader never works the cash out.
2. **Placement.** Not approved: the trade is `rejected` with the Account Manager's code and reason, and nothing is placed. Approved: the Trader places the trade exactly as today (order type, the price range of the ticket, the fill rules of `simulator.py`) and fills at most the approved shares.
3. **Status back, at once.** The moment the trade is `filled` the Trader reports it: `account.record_fill(trade)` (a narrow method over the same logic as `notify_trades`, for ONE trade). The Account Manager applies the fill to the funds and the portfolio and BOOKS it in ONE Postgres transaction (`record_trade`: cash subtracted or added and the position changed together, or neither), then reads the portfolio back from the book, which is the truth, and returns the status of the booking and the new `cash_avail`. A fill the book refuses (the cash does not cover it, the shares are not held, the account is suspended) is NOT a fill: the trade becomes `rejected` with the code `account_refused` and the book's own sentence, and the Trader's `Fill` is not kept. Rule T10 holds: the Trader does not edit what the Account Manager wrote.
4. **Next trade only after that.** The Account Manager has the new available funds; the Trader asks for the approval of the next trade (step 1). No trade is placed before the previous one is booked or refused. This is what stops an overdraw: every purchase meets the funds as the book has them AFTER the earlier trades.

FUNDING (RaiseFunds). Each funding sale is a trade of its own through steps 1 to 3, before its purchase. The purchase asks for its approval only after the book shows the cash the sales brought. The pre-check that nothing is sold when the sales cannot pay for even one share stays (it is an estimate made on the snapshot of prices, before any sale), and the user's consent for the sales stays (a sale is never automatic).

NO ACCOUNT CONNECTED (the desk's JSON portfolio, a backtest, a test): the Account Manager holds the working portfolio ITSELF (`AccountManager.trading_portfolio(portfolio)` stores the one given as its working portfolio, and `record_fill` applies each fill to it with `apply_fill`). It is the Account Manager's, not the Trader's: the same two calls, the same order, no database. `book=False` with a connected account means the same (the fills are applied to the Account Manager's working portfolio and not booked); `book=True` books each fill as above. Backtest never books (as today).

## What changes in the Trader

- `PaperBroker` loses its `portfolio` copy and `apply_fill`: it gets a handle to the Account Manager's two calls (`approve_trade`, `record_fill`) and a read of the sellable shares and the cash only through the Approval. `affordable_shares(cash, ...)` is no longer called with a cash the Trader owns: the approved shares come from the Account Manager (the function may stay as the Account Manager's arithmetic, or move to it: the Trader must not compute the funds).
- `_place` / `_settle` / `_fill` (the one funnel every trade passes through, whoever places it: the model's `place_order` tool or the code's sweep) do steps 1 to 3 around the simulated fill. The model's four tools do not change and still cannot name a symbol, a side or a number of shares; the answer of `place_order` tells the model what the Account Manager said (filled, how many shares, the cash left, or the refusal).
- `execute.py`: step 2 reads the starting portfolio only to REPORT it (`portfolio_before`) and to check the user; the "unbooked fills of an earlier run" step goes (the book already has every fill, since each is booked as it happens); the end-of-run `Notifier` no longer tells anything: it checks the whole run with `work_order.verify_trades` (a finding is reported, and what was booked stays booked: it is the book's, and each trade passed `verify_trade` before it was placed), reads the final portfolio from the Account Manager for `portfolio_after`, and sets each trade's `booked`. `run.portfolio_after` and the cash figures come from the book.
- `Trade` rejection codes (`record.py`, `work_order.py`, `configs/trader_v1.json` where listed) gain `account_refused`; `insufficient_cash` stays (the Account Manager's answer).
- The Trader still reaches the world only through the Account Manager (a test reads its imports): `approve_trade` and `record_fill` are two more of its doors beside `request_prices`, `request_bars`, `trading_portfolio`, `perform_trades`.

## What changes in the Account Manager

`approve_trade(trade, price) -> Approval` and `record_fill(trade) -> Booking` (the status, the problems, the new `cash_avail`), both using the book's own transaction (`record_trade`, `list_portfolio`) and `apply_fill` for the working portfolio; `notify_trades` stays for callers that report a list (it can call `record_fill`). A database failure is the kind of the error only, never connection details, and never an assumption that a trade was booked: a fill the book could not confirm is `rejected` (`account_refused`) and the run stops placing further trades.

## Alternatives considered

- A cycle in the LangGraph desk (`trader -> account_manager_settle -> trader -> ...` once per trade): rejected for now. The desk's graph already hands the order to the Trader node, and the Trader has its own LangGraph for the model's loop; the handshake sits inside the Trader's single placement funnel, so the model's tools, the sweep and the funding all pass through it and no path can skip it. A graph-level cycle would call the model once per trade.
- Keep the Trader's copy and tell the Account Manager at the end (today): rejected by Jacob: two owners of the funds.
- Reserve each purchase's money before any trade: rejected by Jacob: any available funds can be used.

## Amendments of 2026-11-14 (after the sizing move, entries 79 and 80, landed)

1. **The "unbooked fills of an earlier run" step does NOT simply go.** It goes from the TRADER (the Trader keeps no portfolio), but the need stays: when `book` is false (a backtest, a desk JSON portfolio, a saved account run without `--book`) the fills of an earlier run live only on `order.trades`, not in the portfolio the Account Manager hands over. The Account Manager must apply them to its working portfolio itself (through the same `apply_fill` logic as `record_fill`, with nothing booked) before the first approval of a run, so that a second run of the same order sees the cash the first one spent, and a `filled` trade is still final (rule T3). With `book` true the book already has them. Prove it with a test (run the Trader twice on one order, `book=False`).
2. **The per-trade output check.** `work_order.verify_trades` (the guard of step 8, "with a finding nothing is booked") cannot gate a booking that now happens trade by trade. Before a fill is reported to the Account Manager, run the single-trade part of that guard on it (its shape; the decision agrees on record, symbol, side; the shares are not above the decision's; one filled trade per recommendation; a funding trade's `funds_for`). A finding means the trade is NOT reported or booked: reject it with the closest existing reject code (or add one, and say so), put the finding on the run, and stop placing further trades. The end-of-run `verify_trades` stays as a whole-run check that REPORTS (what was booked stays booked: it is the book's).
3. **What the Trader is handed now.** After entries 79 and 80 the decisions on the work order carry the FINAL sized shares (`decision["shares"]`, `decision["size"]`): the Account Manager sized them (Auto with no split asked: round-robin, so each stock has a final share count; the Trader places ONE trade per stock, never one per share). The approval of step 1 may cut the shares further (the cash left), never raise them.
4. **Tests may use the real local Postgres** (it is up and `tests/test_account_book.py` runs against it, 10 passing): create a throwaway account with `scripts`-style helpers the existing tests use, delete it at the end, never touch the three sample accounts. Include a CONCURRENCY test: two threads (each its own `AccountManager` connected to the same throwaway account) try to book purchases that together cost more than the cash; the book accepts one and refuses the other, and the cash is never below zero.
5. **RaiseFunds (decided 2026-11-15, entry 84: the sizing cash stays the available cash and a funding sale is the minimum).** The sizing cash is the available cash, so a decision never needs RaiseFunds today (entry 80). The funding path of the Trader (funding sales placed with their purchase, the user's yes) keeps working as built and is put through the same handshake; do not change what the funding card asks.
6. **The handshake never waits when no trade remains** (Jacob: "the handshaking should not wait if there are no further trades to execute"). Approval is asked only for a trade that is about to be placed. After the LAST trade's `record_fill` is answered the run goes straight to its end: no further `approve_trade`, no pending approval, no polling, sleeping or timeout. With no ticket to place there is no handshake at all (zero calls of `approve_trade` and `record_fill`). A trade refused at approval, or rejected before it fills, causes no `record_fill`. Tests record the real Account Manager's calls: N filled trades give exactly N approvals and N `record_fill` calls and none after the last answer.

7. **Amendment 7 (Jacob, 2026-11-15, design choices entry 82): three reads around one locked commit, no halting, a failed trade is shown and never retried.** It supersedes the part of amendment 2 that says "stop placing further trades" and the stop rule of entry 81.
   - `record_fill` for a run that books is: SELECT the account (`list_portfolio`); COMMIT the trade in ONE transaction (`AccountStore.book_trade`, with row-level `FOR UPDATE` locks on the account row and on EVERY position row of the account, in symbol order, a `lock_timeout`, and a re-read of the account inside the transaction before it commits: cash, the shares of the symbol, every other holding and the trade row must be as the trade says, or the transaction rolls back with `BookingCheckError`); SELECT the account AGAIN on a new connection and read the trade row back (`read_booked`): the trade row is there and the cash and the shares of the symbol are different from the first SELECT. Only then does the Account Manager answer `booked` with `verified` true, and the Trader asks for the next trade.
   - A trade that fails is NOT done: nothing is committed (the book refused; the transaction rolled back; a failed connection after which the account, read again, is unchanged), or it is `unconfirmed` (nobody can settle it from here). Either way the Account Manager shows the user the error and the account as it stands at once (its `screen`: the user talks to the Account Manager only), the Trader marks the trade `rejected` `account_refused` (an output-check finding is `internal_error`), never retries it, and places the NEXT trade of its list; its approval reads the account as the book has it, so nothing can be overdrawn. `PaperBroker.halted` is removed. A funding sale that fails is failed and the others go on; the purchase is approved against the cash the book then shows.
   - The order is `traded` when every permitted trade is final (`filled` or `rejected` with a reason), failed ones included.
   - Tests: real Postgres on throwaway accounts (`tests/test_account_booking_real.py`): the order of the reads, the locks (including a holding that is not the one traded), a booking waiting for a released lock, two bookings at once, the in-transaction rollbacks, the display, no retry; and a Trader run on the real order in which the first booking is lost and the second trade is booked.
